# Thesis Text Analyzer

This application looks at theses as texts. You add the PDF files of bachelor's, master's and other theses, and for each one the application works out simple, traceable figures: how long the thesis is, how long its sentences and words are, how easy it is to read and how varied its vocabulary is. The results appear in a table and in a report with charts. If you wish, you can create an anonymous export file from them for the research team.

Every figure has a page of its own in the list on the left. It explains what the number means and where its limits lie.

## Background

The application was developed in the context of the research group KIWIT (Functions and Consequences of Artificial Intelligence in the Organisation of Science and Higher Education), funded by the German Federal Ministry of Research, Technology and Space (BMFTR) since April 2025. In KIWIT, the University of Oldenburg, Bielefeld University and the Northern Business School Hamburg jointly study what artificial intelligence does in science and in universities.

One of the central questions is: how does generative AI change academic writing? Theses are particularly well suited to pursue it. There are very many of them, they are structured alike, and it is known when they were written. By measuring features such as the length and structure of the texts, one can examine whether, and how strongly, AI-assisted writing tools have left recognisable traces in theses since they became widely available at the end of 2022.

## Privacy

The application works entirely on your own computer and makes no connections to the internet. Data leaves the computer only if you create an export and pass it on yourself. An export contains no names of people, no file names and no text from the theses.

All details are in the [privacy statement](guide:privacy).
